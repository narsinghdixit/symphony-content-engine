# PureFacts Competitive Reference

---

## 1. BILLFIN / REDI2 (ENVESTNET)

**What they do**: Cloud-based advisory billing solution. Acquired by Redi2, which is part of Envestnet. Used by 670+ advisory firms. Handles AUM fee calculations (flat, tiered, banded), invoice generation, and fee splitting.

**Strengths**:
- Easy onboarding for small-to-mid RIAs
- Cost-effective for firms with simple billing needs
- Envestnet ecosystem integration (planning, portfolio management, data aggregation)
- Decent custodian integrations (Schwab, Fidelity, RBC)
- Users report reducing billing from hours/days to ~30 minutes

**Weaknesses**:
- Billing only — no compensation management, no revenue analytics, no pricing governance
- SMB ceiling — struggles with enterprise complexity (multi-entity, tiered households, cross-border)
- No revenue leakage or spillage analytics
- No advisor compensation alignment capability
- Limited audit trail depth for regulatory defense

**Displacement angle**: "BillFin solves billing. PureFacts solves revenue. If your only problem is calculating a fee, BillFin works. If your problem is that you're losing 1–5% of EBITDA and you can't see where, you need a revenue platform."

## 2. SS&C ADVENT

**What they do**: Enterprise investment management suite (portfolio management, accounting, reporting) with billing as one module. Massive installed base. Acquired by SS&C Technologies in 2015.

**Strengths**:
- Brand recognition and trust — decades in the industry
- Full portfolio management stack (Advent APX, Moxy, Geneva)
- Large installed base creates switching costs
- Deep custodian and data integrations
- Pete Hess (PureFacts President) was CEO of Advent for 3 years — PureFacts understands this competitor intimately

**Weaknesses**:
- Billing is a feature, not the product — lacks depth in revenue optimization
- No dedicated compensation management or advisor alignment capabilities
- Rigid architecture — slow to innovate on pricing governance, revenue analytics
- Firms locked into the Advent stack can't easily add best-of-breed revenue tools
- Not purpose-built for the "find, protect, grow" revenue use case

**Displacement angle**: "Advent is a portfolio system with billing bolted on. PureFacts is a revenue system built from the ground up. You don't need to replace Advent — PureFacts integrates alongside it to handle what Advent can't: compensation, pricing governance, revenue analytics, and leakage prevention."

## 3. IN-HOUSE / SPREADSHEETS (THE #1 REAL COMPETITOR)

**What they do**: Custom Excel models, homegrown scripts, manual reconciliation processes. Often built by one person in Operations or Finance who "knows the model." Sometimes supplemented with Access databases or lightly customized billing modules within custodian platforms.

**Why firms stay**:
- Familiarity — "we've always done it this way"
- Perceived control — "we can customize anything"
- Sunk cost — years of building and tweaking
- Fear of migration — "switching will be painful"
- The person who built it is still there (until they leave)

**Weaknesses**:
- Error-prone: one mis-keyed digit repeats across billing cycles undetected
- Unauditable: no approval trails, no data lineage, no version control
- Single point of failure: when the builder leaves, the model becomes a black box
- Cannot scale: adding entities, funds, or advisors means more spreadsheets, not more capacity
- Invisible leakage: the spreadsheet calculates what you tell it to — it cannot show you revenue you're not collecting
- Regulatory risk: SEC and FINRA increasingly test whether firms can demonstrate compliance through records and lineage

**Displacement angle**: "Your spreadsheet can't show you the revenue you're not collecting. It can't prove to an examiner that your fees match your disclosures. And it can't scale when your next acquisition doubles your billing complexity overnight."

## 4. DOING NOTHING (INERTIA)

**Why firms stay**: Change is expensive, other priorities feel more urgent, "we'll get to it next year," the pain isn't acute enough to override the fear of transition.

**The cost of waiting**:
- 1–5% of EBITDA leaks every year the firm delays. On $200M managed revenue, that's up to $10M.
- Regulatory enforcement is accelerating, not slowing. The cost of a fine is 5–10x the visible penalty (the "Invisible Invoice Stack" — remediation, oversight drag, commercial friction, strategic delay).
- Fee compression makes every recovered basis point more valuable. The ROI of fixing revenue operations improves every quarter margins tighten.
- Advisor talent competition is intensifying. Firms without transparent compensation lose recruiting battles.

**Displacement angle**: "Every quarter you wait, 1–5% of EBITDA leaks. That's not a rounding error — it's a strategic choice to leave money on the table."

## 5. OTHER NICHE PLAYERS

### Tegra118 (Advantage Fee)
- Cloud-based billing and revenue management for wealth, custody, and trust firms
- Reasonable enterprise capability but limited market presence
- No compensation management component

### Orion Advisor Technology
- Portfolio management and reporting platform with billing capabilities
- Strong in the RIA segment, but billing is a feature within a broader platform, not the core focus
- No dedicated revenue optimization or compensation management

### Custom builds on Salesforce or custodian platforms
- Some firms attempt to build billing logic within Salesforce or extend custodian-provided billing tools
- Quickly hits complexity ceiling for multi-entity, tiered fee, or cross-border scenarios
- Maintenance burden falls on internal teams

## COMPETITIVE POSITIONING PRINCIPLES

1. **Never bash — reframe.** Don't say "BillFin is bad." Say "BillFin solves a different, smaller problem."
2. **Lead with the gap.** Competitors solve billing. PureFacts solves revenue. The gap between those two things is where 1–5% of EBITDA lives.
3. **Use the "three Cs" framework.** Compression, Collection, Complexity — three forces squeezing economics simultaneously. Point solutions address one. PureFacts addresses all three.
4. **Pete Hess is a differentiator.** The President of PureFacts was the CEO of Advent. That is credibility-by-biography: PureFacts leadership has operated at the heart of the competitive landscape and chose to build something different.
5. **The real competitor is inertia.** Most deals are lost to "do nothing," not to a named competitor. Quantify the cost of waiting.
