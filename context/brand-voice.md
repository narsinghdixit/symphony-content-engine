## 2. PUREFACTS CORPORATE VOICE (blog posts, nurture emails, website copy, one-pagers)

**Style**: Confident, precise, data-driven. Financial services insider tone — speaks the language of the industry, not the language of tech marketing. Shows the math rather than making claims. Problem-first framing: always names the cost of the status quo before introducing the solution.

**Tone**: Authoritative but not condescending. Treats the reader as a peer who is smart but busy. No hype, no breathlessness, no "we're excited to announce." Lets results and data do the persuading.

**Rhythm**: Sentences average 12–18 words. Paragraphs 2–4 sentences for blog/email content. Uses structured formatting (bullets, headers) to support scanning. Statistics always include source context.

**Personality markers**:
- Leads with the problem and its cost in concrete terms
- Uses "firm" or "firms" more than "companies" or "organizations" (financial services convention)
- Prefers "revenue" and "revenue integrity" over generic "growth" or "efficiency"
- Cites specific industry data (EY, FINRA, SEC, Cerulli, McKinsey) rather than vague "studies show"
- Ends blog posts and emails with a specific, actionable CTA — never "learn more" or "click here"

## 4. BANNED PHRASES AND PATTERNS

### Hard-banned words and phrases (never use)
- "delve" / "delving into"
- "tapestry"
- "in today's rapidly evolving landscape"
- "leverage" (as verb meaning "use" — acceptable only in financial context, e.g., "leverage ratio")
- "synergy" / "synergies"
- "paradigm shift"
- "holistic approach" / "holistic solution"
- "cutting-edge"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "unlock the power of"
- "at the end of the day"
- "needless to say"
- "it goes without saying"
- "let that sink in"
- "full stop" (as emphasis)
- "think about it"
- "revolutionary" / "revolutionize"
- "best-in-class" (unless citing a specific ranking)
- "seamless" / "seamlessly"
- "robust" (overused in tech marketing)
- "end-to-end" (acceptable in technical context, not in marketing headlines)

### Pattern bans
- No emojis unless explicitly specified for a particular asset
- No em-dash overuse (maximum 1 per paragraph; prefer commas, periods, or parentheses)
- No starting paragraphs with "So," or "Look," or "Here's the thing"
- No "I think" hedging in opinion pieces (state the view directly)
- No more than one rhetorical question per piece
- No exclamation marks in professional content
- No "Dear [First Name]" in emails (use "Hi [First Name],")
- No starting with "I'm excited to share" or "I'm thrilled to announce"
- No "we're proud to" or "we're pleased to"
- No hashtag spam (maximum 3 hashtags per LinkedIn post, at the end only)

## 5. STYLE RULES

### Sentence and paragraph structure
- Sentence length: vary between 6 and 22 words. Average target: 14. Mix short declarative punches with medium analytical lines.
- Paragraph length: 1–3 sentences for LinkedIn. 2–4 sentences for blog posts and emails. Never exceed 4 sentences in any marketing paragraph.
- Oxford comma: yes, always.

### Numbers and data
- Always include source context for statistics: "(EY, 2024)" or "(FINRA, 2024 fine monies report)"
- Use specific numbers over vague claims. "$13M in annual value" beats "significant cost savings."
- Percentages under 10: spell out ("five percent"). Over 10: use numerals ("85%").
- Dollar amounts: use "$" with numerals. Use "M" for millions, "B" for billions, "T" for trillions. Example: "$15T in AuA"

### CTAs
- Be specific and action-oriented: "Schedule a 20-minute revenue assessment" not "Learn more"
- Use verb-first phrasing: "See how," "Get the data," "Schedule a conversation"
- One CTA per asset (except nurture sequences, which escalate across emails)

### Formatting
- Use markdown headers, bullets, and bold for scannability
- Bold key metrics and stats on first mention in any piece
- Use tables for comparison or multi-item reference content
- Avoid walls of text — if a paragraph could be bullets, make it bullets
